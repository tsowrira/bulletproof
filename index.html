// Approach & Evidence — Why Each Choice

Five principles, tailored to your physiology

Designed around four converging constraints: post-revision microdiscectomy (L4–L5/L5–S1), generalized hypermobility, left ACL history, and training experience that needs progress without flare. Each principle below is supported by published rehab and exercise-science literature.

01 · Spine-Sparing
Hinge, don't flex under load

Cumulative lumbar flexion under load is the dominant mechanism for annular damage. Replace flexion-bias movements (deadlift bottom, hollow body, RDL without pattern) with hinge-pattern teachers (band pull-through), anti-rotation (Pallof), and dead bug.

McGill (2002, 2007, 2015); Adams & Dolan; Callaghan & McGill
02 · Multifidus First
Daily, low-load, high-frequency segmental control

Multifidus atrophies after disc injury and does not automatically rebound. Retraining requires daily, low-load, high-frequency motor-control work — not heavy stabilization. Bird-dog with holds and prone unilateral isometrics are the canonical rebuilders.

Hodges & Richardson (1996, 1999); Hides et al. (1996, 2008)
03 · Hypermobility Protocol
Closed-chain, mid-range, isometric → eccentric → concentric

Hypermobile joints rely on passive structures (ligaments, capsules) when active control fails. Training principle inverts: avoid end-range under load, prioritize co-contraction, build isometric strength first, then slow eccentrics, then concentric. Bands superior to free weights for constant tension.

Russek (1999); Simmonds & Keer (2007); Engelbert et al.; Mottram
04 · Volume That Recovers
10–20 hard sets/wk per muscle, rep-range agnostic

Hypertrophy responds across 5–30 reps when training is taken close to failure. Total weekly hard-set count drives growth; "compound = low reps, isolation = high reps" is a 1RM heuristic, not a hypertrophy law. For post-surgical + ED-recovery context, lower volume = better systemic recovery without strength penalty.

Schoenfeld et al. (2017, 2019); Helms et al.; Morton et al.
05 · Asymmetry & Targeting
Left ACL VMO + L5/S1 distal atrophy get extra reps

Post-ACL VMO atrophy persists for years without targeted work. Asymmetric warmup (25 left / 20 right banded TKE) addresses the structural side. Distal lower-leg work (tibialis posterior + peroneal) targets neurogenic atrophy patterns specific to L5/S1 nerve-root involvement.

Snyder-Mackler; Buckthorpe et al.; Distefano et al.

What are CARs?

Controlled Articular Rotations — slow, deliberate circles that take each joint through its full active range under muscular control. For shoulders: stand tall, one arm at a time, rotate the entire arm in the largest circle possible while keeping the rest of your body completely still. Move as slowly as you can. Pause 2 seconds at the positions that feel sticky or weak. The goal is active control at end range — not passive swinging.

📹 Pull-Up Form — From Your Video

Back looks genuinely strong mid-pull. Two fixes: (1) Elbows flare wide at top — drive to back pockets, moves load from traps to lats. (2) Forward head at peak — chest to bar, not chin. These two changes add reps and drop neck tension within 2 weeks.

Prone Y at 125° — Lower Trap
3 × 10
45s rest
2 · 2 · 3

Setup & Execution
Lie face-down on bench or floor. Arms extended overhead at ~125° abduction (between straight overhead and a T) — forming a wide Y. Thumbs point UP toward ceiling. Lift arms slightly off the surface, squeeze the muscle low between the shoulder blades. Hold 2 counts. Lower over 3.
🎯 Coaching Cue
125° + thumbs-up is the position with the highest documented lower trap activation (~97% MVIC, Reinold/Wilk). Lower trap is the upward rotator your scapulae need — undertrained in almost everyone. Tiny range, huge return. No upper trap firing — chin tucked, neck long.

Reinold & Wilk's EMG study of scapular muscle activation across 12 rehab positions found prone Y at ~125° abduction with thumbs-up generates the highest lower-trap-to-upper-trap activation ratio of any exercise tested. Cools and the Belgian scapular dyskinesis group independently converged on the same finding.

The position selectively biases the lower trap (an upward rotator) over the upper trap (an elevator) — the recruitment pattern hypermobile shoulders specifically need to stabilize during pressing volume, since hypermobile athletes default to upper-trap-dominant strategies.

— Reinold & Wilk (2007); Cools et al. (2014); Kibler & Sciascia

⚠ Hypermobility Caveat — Read Before Each Set

Anterior delt + serratus loading at extreme leverage. Your handstand practice gives you the wrist conditioning, but anterior shoulder capsule is hypermobile — this is the highest anterior-instability risk in the program. Stop a rep the instant you feel pinching or pulling at the front of the shoulder. Hands rotate slightly OUTWARD (fingers ~10 o'clock and 2 o'clock), shoulders forward of wrists, scaps protracted. If form drifts, drop reps before adding any range.

Setup & Execution
Standard push-up start, then walk hands back so wrists are below your hips and fingers point slightly outward. Lean forward — shoulders travel out past wrists. Maintain protracted scaps (push the floor away the entire time, no scap retraction at bottom). Slow 3-count descent, no chest-touching, push back. Body stays one straight line — no sag, no pike.
🎯 Coaching Cue
Push the floor AWAY at the bottom — scaps stay round and forward. Active shoulder fire, never passive. The lean forward is what makes this work — chest staying over hands defeats the purpose.

⚠ Loading Status — Updated May 2026

You're not ready for axial load yet. Conventional barbell deadlifts are permanently shelved. Loaded RDLs and single-leg RDLs are deferred until PT/surgeon clearance — band pull-through is the pattern teacher in the meantime. Trap bar deadlift gating: earliest 9–12 months post-revision, behind functional criteria + clearance.

3 × 10
60s rest
3 · 1 · 2
hinge · stand · drive
Red band — anchor at FLOOR behind you, face away
Setup & Execution
Red band anchored at floor behind. Stand facing away. Step out for tension. Reach between legs, palms toward you. Hinge at hips — push hips back, soft knees, neutral spine. Stand up by driving hips forward (not by squatting up). Glute squeeze finishes the rep — no lumbar extension at top.
🎯 Coaching Cue
This is the central pattern teacher for the entire program. The hip hinge is the structural gap your old program worked around rather than rebuilt. Push hips BACK first, then forward — never down/up. Spine stays one rigid line throughout. Master this for 4 weeks before progressing toward any RDL.

McGill's biomechanics work established that posterior-chain extension under load is safest when motion comes from the hip joint with a neutral spine — not from the lumbar segments. The hip-hinge pattern, taught with light band resistance before any axial load, is the canonical motor-control prerequisite to RDL/deadlift in disc-injury rehab.

Adams & Dolan's annular fiber research is the load-bearing reason this matters for you specifically: bending + compression (the failure mode of a poorly-patterned hinge) produces annular damage at loads far below pure compression. Post-revision, you have less margin than someone with a fully intact annulus — making the pattern non-negotiable, not optional.

— McGill (2007, 2015); Adams & Dolan; Callaghan & McGill; Marshall & Murphy

Setup & Execution
Part 1 · B-Stance Hip Thrust (3 × 12):
1. Upper back on a low bench (~14" — the bench should hit just below your shoulder blades).
2. Working leg flat on the floor. Other leg planted on the heel only, toes pulled up, foot slightly forward — kickstand for balance, not for load.
3. Drive through the working-leg heel. Hips up to a neutral line (knee–hip–shoulder). Ribs DOWN at the top — no posterior pelvic tilt, no lumbar arch.

Part 2 · Single-Leg Banded Hip Thrust (3 × 8 each):
1. Same bench setup. Red band above both knees.
2. Working leg planted. Non-working leg straight up in the air (perpendicular to floor).

4. 3-count hold at top, ribs down. Lower with control.
🎯 Coaching Cue
Glute max work without axial spinal load — exactly what you need right now. The cue is "hips up, ribs down" — they happen simultaneously. Any pelvic tilt at the top means glutes lost the rep and lumbar took over. End the set.

Nordic Hamstring Curl — Machine or Band Assist
3 × 5
90s rest
5 · 3 · 2
5s down · 3s ISO · assist up
Setup & Execution — Bilateral
Both legs lower together — Nordic is bilateral; feet anchored, knees the only joint moving. Tall neutral spine, ribs locked down. Lower toward floor over 5 full seconds, stay rigid. Use assist to return upright. Stop 15° before full knee extension on the return — never lock out.

Two assist options (pick what's available):
· Machine + 10 lb plate assist (your current setup) — counterweight pulley with a 10 lb plate. Cleanest progression because you can drop the plate weight by 2.5 lb increments as you get stronger.
· Black band around waist, anchored behind — fallback when the machine isn't available.
🎯 Coaching Cue
Premier hamstring injury prevention exercise. For hypermobile knees: stopping before full extension means the muscle catches you, not the joint capsule. The 5-second eccentric is all the adaptation — never rush it. The machine + plate setup is gold because the assist is constant and quantified, unlike a band that stiffens nonlinearly.

Petersen's landmark RCT in elite Danish soccer players cut hamstring injury rate by ~51% and recurrence by ~85% over a season with eccentric Nordic protocols. Subsequent meta-analyses (van Dyk; Al Attar) confirmed the mechanism: hamstring fascicle length increases under eccentric overload, shifting the force-length curve into a more protective range.

For your specific case — left ACL history + hypermobile knees — the eccentric Nordic also creates co-contraction signal across the posterior knee that supports the reconstructed ligament. The "stop 15° before extension" rule keeps load on muscle rather than joint capsule, which matters disproportionately for hypermobile athletes.

— Petersen et al. (2011); van Dyk et al. (2019); Mendiguchia et al.; Al Attar et al.

What are CARs?

Controlled Articular Rotations — slow, deliberate circles that take each joint through its full active range under muscular control. For shoulders: stand tall, one arm at a time, rotate the entire arm in the largest circle possible while keeping the rest of your body completely still. Move as slowly as you can. // Multifidus + Glute Med + Decompression · Every Day

Three movements every day, low-load, high-frequency. The rest of the daily menu (side-lying multifidus lift, hip CARs, chin nods, SLR nerve glide) rotates 3×/week to avoid skip-fatigue.

Bird-Dog with Holds
3 × 8 each side · 5–10s hold at full extension
Multifidus + anti-rotation core. Reach long, hips completely level, zero rotation. Hold the extended position before returning. Single highest-priority unaddressed deficit at your stage — multifidus does not auto-recover after disc injury and needs daily, low-load, high-frequency work specifically.— Hodges & Richardson (1996, 1999); Hides et al.; McGill
Banded Clamshell at 30° Flexion
3 × 15 each side
Yellow band above knees. Knees forward of hip line (~30° hip flexion). Open knees against band, hips stacked, no rolling backward. The 30° flexion angle is the load-bearing detail: glute-med activation is high while TFL stays quiet — exactly inverted at neutral hip flexion.— Distefano et al. (2009); Selkowitz et al.
Passive Hang from Swedish Ladder
10–30s × 2/day
Genuine spinal traction — no muscle work, just hang. Different exercise from active dead hang. Replaces the "reverse hyper" testimonial claim with actual decompression. Disc tissue rehydrates under low-magnitude unloading; this is the evidence-grounded version of the spinal-traction promise.— Belavy et al. (2016); Adams & Roughley
Rotate 3×/week: side-lying multifidus lift · prone unilateral isometrics · quadruped hip CARs · standing banded hip flexion above 90° (psoas/iliacus) · supine chin nods (Jull craniocervical flexion protocol) · straight-leg raise nerve glide.
// Deferred — Not Now
Single-leg RDL (any variant): asymmetric hip hinge under spine engagement — re-herniation mechanism. Re-evaluate after 12 weeks of clean band pull-through and PT clearance.
Loaded RDL: deferred — band pull-through is the pattern teacher in the meantime.
Conventional barbell deadlift: permanently shelved.
Trap bar deadlift: earliest 9–12 months post-revision, 5 functional criteria + surgeon clearance.
Reverse hyperextension: momentum-driven end-range extension is wrong direction for an annular defect. Use passive hang for decompression.
Hollow body hold: creates lumbar flexion under load. Pallof and dead bug cover the core demand without it.
Curtsy lunge: transverse-plane knee shear + lumbar rotation — wrong cost/benefit for ACL + hypermobility + post-revision. B-stance hip thrust + Spanish squat + clamshell cover the targets.
Passive static stretching: more passive ROM without control = more instability for hypermobile joints.
// Non-Negotiable Rules

HYPERMOBILITY PROTOCOL

Joint hypermobility means the nervous system has learned to rely on passive structures — ligaments and joint capsules — instead of muscles. Every rule below actively retrains that pattern toward active stabilization.

Never Lock Out

Stop 10–15° before full extension — elbows, knees, hips. The click is capsular loading, not muscular control. That distinction matters for everything.

Warmup Every Session

Hypermobile joints need neural activation before load. This is where hypermobile athletes get hurt. No exceptions.

Proprioception Work

Eyes-closed balance in every leg warmup retrains position sense. Add random perturbation when possible. Root-cause injury prevention.

Isometrics Are Medicine

Every hold in this program teaches muscles to fire without passive end-range joint reliance. The core retraining goal.

No Passive Stretching

No yin yoga. No passive splits. No hanging end-range. More passive range without control = more instability.

Tendons Take 8+ Weeks

Muscles adapt in 2–3 weeks. Tendons 8+. When something feels easy, wait 4 more weeks before adding load.

Closed-Chain Mid-Range

All hypertrophy progressions: closed-chain, mid-range, isometric → eccentric → concentric. Co-contraction cues, not feeling-the-stretch cues.

HRV in Trends Only

Use HRV as own-baseline trends, not absolute readiness scores. Day-to-day variance is noise.
