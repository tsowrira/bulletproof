PULL & POSTERIOR CHAIN
Lower lat width · Rear delt · Lower trap · Bicep peak · Glute-ham tie-in · Dead hang
⚡ Warmup — 11 min
3 minSpin bike — easy pace, zero resistance. Elevate heart rate and warm the joints before upper body work.
2 minShoulder CARs — full slow circles each arm independently. Pause 2s at every end position.
What are CARs?

Controlled Articular Rotations — slow, deliberate circles that take each joint through its full active range under muscular control. For shoulders: stand tall, one arm at a time, rotate the entire arm in the largest circle possible while keeping the rest of your body completely still. Move as slowly as you can. Pause 2 seconds at the positions that feel sticky or weak. The goal is active control at end range — not passive swinging.

1 minScapular shrugs on bar — dead hang only, elevate and depress. No pulling yet.
1 minBand pull-aparts × 20 — yellow band, chest height, external rotation throughout.
2 minCat-cow × 10 on all fours — neutral to slight arch only. Zero deep spinal flexion.
1 minWrist circles × 10 each direction + finger splays × 10.
1 minActive hang — scaps packed and depressed. Not passive. Feel the difference.
📹 Pull-Up Form — From Your Video

Back looks genuinely strong mid-pull. Two fixes: (1) Elbows flare wide at top — drive to back pockets, moves load from traps to lats. (2) Forward head at peak — chest to bar, not chin. These two changes add reps and drop neck tension within 2 weeks.

Setup & Execution
Lie face-down on bench or floor. Arms extended overhead at ~125° abduction (between straight overhead and a T) — forming a wide Y. Thumbs point UP toward ceiling. Lift arms slightly off the surface, squeeze the muscle low between the shoulder blades. Hold 2 counts. Lower over 3.
🎯 Coaching Cue
125° + thumbs-up is the position with the highest documented lower trap activation (~97% MVIC, Reinold/Wilk). Lower trap is the upward rotator your scapulae need — undertrained in almost everyone. Tiny range, huge return. No upper trap firing — chin tucked, neck long.

3 × 6

⚠ Hypermobility Caveat — Read Before Each Set

Anterior delt + serratus loading at extreme leverage. Your handstand practice gives you the wrist conditioning, but anterior shoulder capsule is hypermobile — this is the highest anterior-instability risk in the program. Stop a rep the instant you feel pinching or pulling at the front of the shoulder. Hands rotate slightly OUTWARD (fingers ~10 o'clock and 2 o'clock), shoulders forward of wrists, scaps protracted. If form drifts, drop reps before adding any range.

Setup & Execution
Standard push-up start, then walk hands back so wrists are below your hips and fingers point slightly outward. Lean forward — shoulders travel out past wrists. Maintain protracted scaps (push the floor away the entire time, no scap retraction at bottom). Slow 3-count descent, no chest-touching, push back. Body stays one straight line — no sag, no pike.
🎯 Coaching Cue
Push the floor AWAY at the bottom — scaps stay round and forward. Active shoulder fire, never passive. The lean forward is what makes this work — chest staying over hands defeats the purpose.
📈 Path Forward
NowKnees down, slight forward lean, 3×6
Wk 4Full plank, mild lean, 3×6
Wk 8Pronounced lean — shoulders well past wrists, 3×5

LEGS & SPINAL ARMOR
Hip hinge pattern · Quad balance · Glute density (axial-unloaded) · VMO · L5/S1 ankle work · McGill
⚠ Loading Status — Updated May 2026

You're not ready for axial load yet. Conventional barbell deadlifts are permanently shelved. Loaded RDLs and single-leg RDLs are deferred until PT/surgeon clearance — band pull-through is the pattern teacher in the meantime. Trap bar deadlift gating: earliest 9–12 months post-revision, behind functional criteria + clearance.

⚡ Warmup — 18 min
3 minSpin bike — easy pace. Warms lower body and lubricates hips and knees before leg work.
3 minMcGill Big 3 activation — curl-up × 5, side plank × 20s each, bird dog × 5 each. Fires the core before any leg loading.
2 minHip 90/90 — internal and external rotation both sides. Slow, controlled. No forcing end range.
2 minBanded clamshells × 20 each side at 30° hip flexion (knees forward of hip line) — yellow band above knees. Favorable glute-med-to-TFL ratio at this angle.
2 minBodyweight glute bridge × 15 — full hip extension, zero lumbar hyperextension at top.
2 minBanded TKE — asymmetric: 25 reps left leg / 20 reps right. Yellow band looped around something solid behind the knee, step away to tension, drive knee straight into extension. Asymmetric volume targets left ACL VMO deficit.
2 minSingle-leg balance — eyes open 30s then eyes closed 30s, each leg. Bonus: have someone tap you randomly during eyes-closed (perturbation).
1 minAnkle circles × 10 each direction + toe spreading × 10.
1 minSciatic nerve glide × 10 each leg — supine, extend leg straight, flex foot (toes toward shin) for 3s. PT-prescribed.
Band Pull-Through — Hip Hinge Teacher
3 × 10
60s rest
3 · 1 · 2
hinge · stand · drive
Red band — anchor at FLOOR behind you, face away
Setup & Execution
Red band anchored at floor behind. Stand facing away. Step out for tension. Reach between legs, palms toward you. Hinge at hips — push hips back, soft knees, neutral spine. Stand up by driving hips forward (not by squatting up). Glute squeeze finishes the rep — no lumbar extension at top.
🎯 Coaching Cue
This is the central pattern teacher for the entire program. The hip hinge is the structural gap your old program worked around rather than rebuilt. Push hips BACK first, then forward — never down/up. Spine stays one rigid line throughout. Master this for 4 weeks before progressing toward any RDL.

⚠ Hypermobility Cap

Cap depth at parallel — do not chase full pistol depth. Hypermobile knees give up structural support past parallel. Tap height never below the height where pelvis stays neutral. Watch left knee for inward cave on every rep — band above knees if needed.

Setup & Execution
Stand on step platform next to the Swedish ladder. For LEFT leg sets — position your left side about 2 inches from a vertical rung. Descend without touching it. Fingertips available if needed. 4-count descent. Non-working leg extends forward. Lower until heel taps OR until you reach parallel — whichever comes first. Stop at first sign of butt wink or knee cave.
🎯 Coaching Cue
Deepest position where pelvis stays completely neutral — that's the target, not deepest possible. Butt wink = lumbar flexion = stop that rep. Watch the left knee — if it caves toward midline, end the set.

Wk 4Fingertips no longer needed for balance
Wk 8Hold longer at bottom — same depth cap

⚠ Strict Form Rules — Read Before Each Set

Partial ROM only. Kneeling on pad. Ribs down, glutes locked the entire descent — non-negotiable. Lean back ONLY as far as you can keep zero terminal lumbar hyperextension. SWIM — ACTIVE RECOVERY
Zero spinal compression · Lat volume · VO2 max · Recovery from Wed legs · Skip-friendly placement
// Skip Logic

Swim is positioned here so it can be skipped without breaking the lift schedule. If you skip: a 20-min walk + the daily low-load menu covers active recovery. If you've been skipping recently, that's fine — Wed legs and Fri skill carry the structural load.

Session Structure (45–55 min)
Warmup — easy freestyle, find rhythm 300m
Drill — catch-up drill, focus on catch 4 × 50m
Main set — choose one below 600–800m
Pull buoy — arms only, no kick 4 × 50m
Kick set — flutter board, neutral neck 4 × 25m
Cooldown — backstroke, easy 200m
Main Set — Rotate Weekly
Freestyle build — easy/med/hard per 25m 6 × 100m
Negative split — 2nd 50 faster than 1st 4 × 100m
Backstroke focus — thoracic extension 8 × 50m
Mixed — alternate free and back per lap 400m
Volume Progression
Now1100–1200 yards total
Wk 4Add one 100m set to main set
Wk 81500 yards target — add pull buoy set length
Spine & Form Rules
Freestyle rotation from hips — never the neck. Head neutral on breathing. Pull buoy set = lat pulldown with zero spinal load — lats fire maximally here. Backstroke actively extends and decompresses thoracic spine. Flip turns fine — tuck tight, push off both feet evenly. Kickboard: face-down if possible, neutral neck.
⏳ Building Toward These

No butterfly — repeated lumbar hyperextension under load. Caution on breaststroke — hip drop at glide creates lumbar extension. Goals, not limits. Revisit both at 12-month mark when your doctor gives the green light.

Why This Works for Your Goals
Freestyle pull = lat pulldown with zero joint stress. Water provides isokinetic resistance — constant tension through full range. VO2 max gains extend dead hang time and pull-up endurance directly. Swimming is one of the most evidence-backed post-discectomy modalities. Thursday placement is deliberate: recovery from Wed legs, skip-friendly on busy weeks.

What are CARs?

Controlled Articular Rotations — slow, deliberate circles that take each joint through its full active range under muscular control. For shoulders: stand tall, one arm at a time, rotate the entire arm in the largest circle possible while keeping the rest of your body completely still. Move as slowly as you can. Pause 2 seconds at the positions that feel sticky or weak. The goal is active control at end range — not passive swinging.

Setup & Execution

// Non-Negotiable Rules

HYPERMOBILITY PROTOCOL

Joint hypermobility means the nervous system has learned to rely on passive structures — ligaments and joint capsules — instead of muscles. Every rule below actively retrains that pattern toward active stabilization.

Never Lock Out

Stop 10–15° before full extension — elbows, knees, hips. The click is capsular loading, not muscular control. That distinction matters for everything.

Warmup Every Session

Hypermobile joints need neural activation before load. This is where hypermobile athletes get hurt. No exceptions.

Proprioception Work

Eyes-closed balance in every leg warmup retrains position sense. Add random perturbation when possible. Root-cause injury prevention.

Isometrics Are Medicine

Every hold in this program teaches muscles to fire without passive end-range joint reliance. The core retraining goal.

No Passive Stretching

No yin yoga. No passive splits. No hanging end-range. More passive range without control = more instability.

Tendons Take 8+ Weeks

Muscles adapt in 2–3 weeks. Tendons 8+. When something feels easy, wait 4 more weeks before adding load.

Closed-Chain Mid-Range

All hypertrophy progressions: closed-chain, mid-range, isometric → eccentric → concentric. Co-contraction cues, not feeling-the-stretch cues.

HRV in Trends Only

Use HRV as own-baseline trends, not absolute readiness scores. Day-to-day variance is noise.
