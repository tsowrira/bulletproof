// Approach & Evidence — Why Each Choice

Five principles, tailored to your physiology

Designed around five converging constraints: post-revision lumbar microdiscectomy, generalized hypermobility, left ACL history, a patellofemoral knee that flares with the wrong loading, and a return to training that needs progress without flare. Machines, bands, and bodyweight are each chosen per movement for whichever is safest and most effective — not by dogma. Every principle below is supported by published rehab and exercise-science literature.

01 · Spine-Sparing
Hinge, don't flex under load

Cumulative lumbar flexion under load is the dominant mechanism for annular damage. Replace flexion-bias movements (deadlift bottom, hollow body, RDL without pattern) with hinge-pattern teachers (light hand-held hinge, band pull-through as form-check), anti-rotation (Pallof), and dead bug.

McGill (2002, 2007, 2015); Adams & Dolan; Callaghan & McGill
02 · Multifidus First
Daily, low-load, high-frequency segmental control

Multifidus atrophies after disc injury and does not automatically rebound. Retraining requires daily, low-load, high-frequency motor-control work — not heavy stabilization. Bird-dog with holds and prone unilateral isometrics are the canonical rebuilders.

Hodges & Richardson (1996, 1999); Hides et al. (1996, 2008)
03 · Hypermobility Protocol
Closed-chain, mid-range, isometric → eccentric → concentric

Hypermobile joints lean on ligaments and capsules when active control fails. Inverted training rules: no end-range under load, prioritize co-contraction, build isometric → eccentric → concentric. Bands beat free weights for constant tension.

Russek (1999); Simmonds & Keer (2007); Engelbert et al.; Mottram
04 · Volume That Recovers
Start below MEV, climb to 10–20 hard sets/wk per muscle

Returning after time off, the tendon is the rate-limiter, not the muscle — volume starts low (~6 sets/muscle/wk) and climbs one set every two weeks early on, not every week. Hypertrophy responds across 5–30 reps near failure; going to failure isn't required (RIR 1–4 is plenty) and costs more recovery. Machines carry the loadable leg work — load climbs in clean increments at low spinal cost.

Schoenfeld (2017, 2019); proximity-to-failure meta (2023); volume dose-response meta-regression (2024); Israetel landmarks
05 · Asymmetry & Targeting
The weak side earns extra volume — right glute, left VMO, distal foot

The right glute (post-surgical atrophy) gets the 2-1 method: two unilateral sets right for every one left, until symmetry returns. Left-ACL VMO atrophy persists for years without targeted work — asymmetric warmup (25 left / 20 right TKE) plus pain-free leg extension addresses it. Distal lower-leg work targets the nerve-recovery atrophy pattern. And hip/glute-med strength is knee medicine — it beats knee-only work for patellofemoral pain.

Contreras 2-1 method; Snyder-Mackler; Buckthorpe et al.; Halabi et al. (2025) hip+knee for PFP

What are CARs?

Controlled Articular Rotations — slow, deliberate circles that take each joint through its full active range under muscular control. For shoulders: stand tall, one arm at a time, rotate the entire arm in the largest circle possible while keeping the rest of your body completely still. Move as slowly as you can. Pause 2 seconds at the positions that feel sticky or weak. The goal is active control at end range — not passive swinging.

Setup & Execution
Lie face-down on bench or floor. Arms extended overhead at ~125° abduction (between straight overhead and a T) — forming a wide Y. Thumbs point UP toward ceiling. Lift arms slightly off the surface, squeeze the muscle low between the shoulder blades. Hold 2 counts. Lower over 3.
🎯 Coaching Cue

Reinold & Wilk's EMG study of scapular muscle activation across 12 rehab positions found prone Y at ~125° abduction with thumbs-up generates the highest lower-trap-to-upper-trap activation ratio of any exercise tested (specific MVIC numbers vary by source; the LT-to-UT ratio is the robust finding, not the absolute number). Cools and the Belgian scapular dyskinesis group independently converged on the same finding.

The position selectively biases the lower trap (an upward rotator) over the upper trap (an elevator) — the recruitment pattern hypermobile shoulders specifically need to stabilize during pressing volume, since hypermobile athletes default to upper-trap-dominant strategies.

— Reinold & Wilk (2007); Cools et al. (2014); Kibler & Sciascia

Yellow band — anchored to a rung behind you at chest height. One end in working hand.
Setup & Execution
Stand facing away from anchor. Band end in working hand at shoulder height, elbow tucked, palm in. Punch the hand straight forward at shoulder height — arm extends, scapula slides forward AROUND the rib cage (protraction). Reach an inch past full extension — that final inch is the serratus contraction. 1-count pause. Slow 3-count return, scap travels BACK along the rib cage. Opposite hand on the same-side rib to feel the scap glide.
🎯 Coaching Cue
Serratus = scap protraction + upward rotation — the muscle that wraps your ribs and stabilizes overhead positions (handstand, overhead press). Zero anterior-shoulder load, unlike pseudo-planche. The "reach" past full extension is the entire exercise — first 90% of the punch is shoulder, last 10% is serratus.

Decker et al.'s EMG study (1999) and Ekstrom et al. (2003) both rank serratus punch / protraction-emphasis exercises among the top serratus anterior activators per unit of joint load. Critically, scapular protraction is the serratus's primary action — pressing/pushing is secondary. So an isolated punch produces more serratus EMG per joule of shoulder stress than push-up variants.

Why this replaces pseudo-planche for your shoulder: Russek (1999) and Mottram on hypermobility-specific shoulder programming explicitly contraindicate max-leverage anterior loading positions. Pseudo-planche puts shoulders out past wrists with anterior capsule under tensile stress — exactly the position the literature says to avoid in gJHM. This delivers the serratus stimulus pseudo-planche was meant to provide, with the anterior capsule fully unloaded.

— Decker et al. (1999); Ekstrom et al. (2003); Russek (1999); Mottram on gJHM shoulder rehab

⚠ Form Rules — Tighter Than the Band Version

Plates in your hands compress the spine on the way down (the band did not). Two non-negotiables: (1) ROM cap — stop hinging the instant you feel any tension at the back of your legs OR sense the spine wanting to round, whichever first. Do not chase depth. (2) Anti-lateral-flexion — both shoulders stay perfectly level. Suitcase load demands you resist asymmetric drop. If one shoulder dips, you've turned a hinge into a side-bend. Reset.

Setup & Execution
1. Setup: One 5 lb plate in each hand, suitcase grip (palms facing thighs). Feet hip-width, soft knees, ribs DOWN, shoulders pulled back without arching.
2. Hinge: Push hips BACK first — not down. Plates slide down the FRONT of your thighs toward the knees, never further. Hamstrings load like a stretched band.
3. Stop point: first hint of hamstring tension, or first inclination of the spine to round, whichever comes first. For most people that is somewhere between the kneecap and mid-shin. Do not chase your toes.
4. Stand: drive hips forward, glutes squeeze hard at the top. Plates slide back up the thighs.
5. Top: ribs DOWN, no posterior pelvic tilt, no lumbar extension. Reset 1 count before next rep.
🎯 Coaching Cue
Plate ride down your thigh is the cue — if it leaves your thigh, spine is rounding (= butt wink starting). No butt wink at the bottom. PT moved you band → plates because you earned the upgrade and need the motor pattern in a compressive context. Form-check option: any session where mechanics feel off, do one set with a broomstick on your back — three-point contact (head, mid-back, sacrum) touching = spine neutral.

McGill (2007, 2015) established the hip-hinge pattern with neutral spine as the canonical motor-control prerequisite to any axial loading post-disc injury. Progression from dowel/light load to heavier load follows the standard rehab loading curve. Your PT's specific progression (2 lb dowel → 5 lb in clinic, no pain) is textbook graded exposure.

Why the plate version raises the bar from the band version: Adams & Dolan's annular research shows damage occurs at bending + compression at loads far below pure compression alone. The band pull-through delivered an unloading vector (zero compression). Plates in hands deliver mild compression on the descent. Both are safe with neutral spine; the plate version is only safe with a tighter ROM cap because you no longer have the band's posterior assistance to bail out a marginal rep.

Why no goblet/dumbbell-at-chest yet: a chest-held load shifts the center of mass anteriorly, increasing extensor moment at L4–L5 (Cholewicki & McGill). Suitcase plates keep the load vector close to the body's COM line. Goblet hinge is in your progression — at Wk 12, after the plate version is dialed.

— McGill (2007, 2015); Adams & Dolan; Cholewicki & McGill; Schoenfeld (2018) hip-dominant exercise activation review; Hayden et al. (2021) Cochrane post-disc exercise review

Setup & Execution
Kneel on the Nordic pad. Ankles secured by the machine pulley (or anchored under a heavy bench). Body is one rigid line — head, hips, knees stacked. Tip forward from the knees over 5 full seconds — knees extend, hips stay locked in line with the body (no hip bend). The torso travels toward the floor as a single unit. Use your arms to push back upright (machine assist counterweight does most of this). Stop 15° before the knees reach full extension on the way back up — never lock out.

Assist setup: Nordic machine + 10 lb counterweight plate (preferred — drops by 2.5 lb increments as you get stronger). Fallback if no machine: black band looped around waist, anchored to a high bar behind you.
🎯 Coaching Cue

Daily Low-Load
// Multifidus + Glute Med + Decompression · Every Day · ~8 minutes

Three movements every day, low-load, high-frequency. The rest of the daily menu (side-lying multifidus lift, hip CARs, chin nods, SLR nerve glide) rotates 3×/week to avoid skip-fatigue.

⏰ When to Do These — Morning, Before the Day Pulls You

Default slot is first thing in the morning, before coffee or scrolling, while listening to a podcast or just waking up. Why morning:

  • Multifidus benefits from frequency, not intensity — earlier in the day means it actually happens. Evening "I'll do it after dinner" routines drop off fast (compliance research is brutal on this).
  • Bird-dog primes hip-to-shoulder connection for the rest of the day — useful even on non-training days.
  • Decompression hang reverses overnight disc-water-loss (your discs hydrate flat, lose hydration upright; ~15s of hang in the morning is the cheapest version of "morning back routine").

Sequence (~8 min total): wake up → bathroom → bird-dog on the floor (2 min) → clamshell (1 min) → walk to the Swedish ladder for decompression hang (15s × 2, with 30s walk between) → done. Second hang any time before bed.

If the morning doesn't happen: any other slot still beats skipping. The hierarchy is "done sometime" > "done in the right slot" > "done perfectly."

FRAMEWORK · FLARE · GOALS · RECOVERY

The body is resilient and the program is built on that. The cards below give you a plan for the rare days the body sends a new signal, a clear path to unlock the deferred goals, and recovery anchors (sleep, deload, fuel) that compound over months.

💬 When to Loop in Your PT

If something feels meaningfully new — new sensation down a leg, new numbness or weakness — message your PT and get on the schedule. That's the whole rule. Your PT already knows your full picture; they're the right first call, not Google.

⤓ Back Acting Up — A Plan, So You Don't Have to Decide Mid-Flare
Mild — annoying but no new leg signals

Switch to Daily Low-Load only for 3 days. Walk 20+ min/day. Day 4: try the daily routine — if back to baseline, resume Wed Legs A at 50% volume and rebuild over 3 sessions.

Sharper — interrupting sleep or daily stuff

Pause training. Daily walking + decompression hang only. If it's still loud past 48 hours or escalating, ping PT. The "just push through" instinct is the wrong one — three quiet days protects the next three months.

🧠 HOW YOUR BACK ACTUALLY WORKS

Two surgeries means the disc has been mechanically vulnerable. It does not mean your back is fragile in the way the catastrophic injury feels suggest. Three things are simultaneously true and worth holding together:

  • Discs heal. The annular fibers form scar tissue over 6–12 months post-surgery; healed disc tissue is not the same as never-injured disc tissue, but it is meaningfully resilient. Adams & Roughley's nutrition work documents the timeline.
  • Multifidus re-recruits. Post-disc multifidus atrophy is real and persistent — but daily, low-load, high-frequency motor-control work (bird-dog, dead bug) demonstrably restores recruitment over weeks to months (Hodges & Richardson; Hides et al.).
  • Pain ≠ damage in the chronic phase. By 6+ months post-surgery, pain signal often outlives tissue threat. Louw et al.'s meta-analyses on pain neuroscience education show medium-to-large effects on fear-of-load and catastrophizing — relevant because fear-of-load is its own training limiter.

Hold this alongside the Red Flag list. The framework is "trust the trajectory + respect the signals" — both at once, neither alone.

📊 Monthly Test Battery — Track What the Program Is Actually Doing

First Saturday of every month, ~20 minutes. Log in the same note. No interpretation needed mid-test — write the numbers, look at the trend at the 3-month mark. Three months of data is when patterns become visible. Don't act on a single month's reading.

  1. McGill side-plank endurance, L vs R. Hold to failure, both sides. Target: L = R within 5%, both > 60s. R/L difference > 15% is the flag.
  2. McGill back-extensor endurance (Biering-Sørensen). Hold prone-extension over a bench/table to failure. Target: > 90s. Below 60s is the persistent-LBP flag in McGill's data.
  3. Single-leg balance, eyes closed. 30s target each leg. L < R by > 5s = ACL-side proprioceptive deficit still active.
  4. Y-Balance Test, lower quarter (anterior reach). Stand on one leg, reach the other foot forward, mark distance. Anterior reach asymmetry > 4 cm is the rehab-literature flag (Plisky; Smith).
  5. Single-leg sit-to-stand × 5. From a chair, one leg, no hand assist. L < R by > 20% = quad/glute deficit on ACL side.
  6. Joint-position-replication test, knee. Sit on a table edge, eyes closed. Move knee to a target angle, return, repeat. Off by > 10° = proprioceptive flag (Russek 2024).

◐ Temporary-Restriction Overlay — When the Body Needs a Quieter Week or Two

Life produces 1–4 week windows where the standard program needs to dial back: clinician-managed phases, recovery from a minor procedure, medication changes, travel illness, etc. This card is the generic decision tree — substitute the specifics when relevant. The clinician (or the body) sets the dates; this card sets the menu.

Phase 1 — Heading Into It (~7 days before)

Standard program runs. Two small shifts: drop new 1RM testing and aggressive eccentric overload (hold the prior 4 weeks' loads). Spend the runway on technique polish, not PRs. Sleep + protein adequacy are the highest-leverage levers heading in.

Phase 2 — Quietest Window (~10–14 days)

The window where heavy bracing, inversion, and high-impact work are temporarily off the table. Background reason: Valsalva drives intra-abdominal pressure to 100–200 mmHg (Hackett & Chow 2013), and when there's any reason to keep that down, the lifts that demand it have to come out.

OUT: heavy hip thrust · BSS · loaded hinge · Nordic · pull-ups + dead hangs (Valsalva + traction) · wall handstand (inversion) · running · jumping · plyometrics · HIIT · spin bike · flip turns · machine ab/adduction at full ROM (pelvic rotation) · twisting / lateral-flexion under load.

KEEP: walking 30–45 min/day. Continuous-lap swim with open turns only. Daily Low-Load. Light upper-body band work (face pull, prone Y, banded row) at ≤50% RPE, no breath-holding. Half-kneeling / supported variants only. Moderate exercise during these windows is well-supported by the trials available (e.g. ASRM 2024 RCT) — the framing is "drop intensity, keep moving."

Phase 3 — First 5 Days After the Peak

Day 0–2: walking only, 10–20 min, flat ground. Nothing over 5 lb. Day 3–5: extend walking to 30 min, reintroduce supine Daily Low-Load (bird-dog + dead bug only — skip side plank, lateral compression is the limiter). Cap at 10 lb, no breath-holding, no inversions, no swimming if there's any infection-window consideration.

Phase 4 — Ramp Back (2–6 weeks)

Rule of thumb: resume strenuous training only when the body has clearly settled back to its baseline (e.g. cycle resumed as normal, no lingering soreness, sleep back to baseline).

Wk 2: full Daily Low-Load. Bodyweight squats, banded glute, banded row, knee push-ups. No Valsalva, no eccentric-emphasis. RPE cap 6.

Wk 3: reintroduce hinge teacher, goblet squat at prior load −30%, hip thrust at prior load −30%. Swim resumes (open turns). RPE cap 7.

Wk 4+: reintroduce Nordic (top half first), pull-up negatives at 5s eccentric (not the prior 8s), BSS bodyweight → 5 lb → 10 lb. Inversions last. Full prior loads by week 5–6 if no flare.

Supplement overlay for the window

Creatine: stay on at 5 g/day — well-tolerated and useful for cognitive + recovery markers across phases. (Only change if a clinician specifically suggests it for a unique context.)

CoQ10 ubiquinol: keep on at 200–400 mg continuously. Mitochondrial support that doesn't need to come off for any phase.

Vitamin D: keep at 2000–4000 IU; no phase-specific bolus.

NAC (optional): 1.8 g/day from start to end of the active window has been associated with better recovery markers in some clinical contexts (Cheraghi 2016, 2022). Clinician-gated, not a default.

Iron: recheck serum ferritin periodically — vegetarian athletes are prone to silent depletion, and any hormonal, dietary, or training change can shift it.

When in doubt in Phase 2 or 3, the default answer is "walk + Daily Low-Load only" — the always-safe floor.

🌙 SLEEP & ENERGY AVAILABILITY
Sleep — single biggest recovery lever

Target 7.5–9h/night. Dark room, no screens 30 min pre-sleep. Track weekly average, not nightly variance. Dattilo 2011 + Knowles 2018: sleep is the single largest hypertrophy + tissue-healing variable. More important than any supplement.

Energy availability — eat enough for the work

1500 kcal is the low edge for a small-framed active female. Cycle regularity is a downstream signal — if cycle goes irregular during a high-volume training block, energy availability is too low. Add 200–300 kcal/day and recheck in 4–6 weeks (Mountjoy et al. on RED-S).

⚙ Per-Tissue Progression Speeds — overrides the calendar

Muscle adapts in ~3 weeks; tendon takes 8+ (Bohm 2015 meta; Magnusson 2008). Lifts where the limiter is tendon get a 2-mesocycle progression cadence (12 weeks per load step) instead of 6.

  • Tendon-limited (12-wk per step): Nordic curl, pull-up 6s negatives, serratus-punch overhead progression, hand-held hinge load steps, dead hang time. Load or assist changes happen at wk 12 (start of mesocycle 3), not wk 4 or 8 within a mesocycle.
  • Muscle-limited (6-wk per step): banded rows, kneeling pulldown, hip thrust load, BSS load (PT gate-permitting), banded press, Pallof.
  • Motor-control / skill (continuous): handstand wall holds, shoulder taps, dead bug, bird-dog, balance work. No deload — just don't add holds during deload week. These are nervous-system, not tissue-stress.

Practical: when a Wk 4 / Wk 8 / Wk 12 step appears on a tendon-limited card, read it as "start of mesocycle 1 / 2 / 3," not as calendar weeks. The Nordic 7.5 lb → 5 lb → 2.5 lb path runs across 18 weeks of training, not 12.

📡 Autoregulation — Performance Signals Beat Wearable Scores

Wearable readiness scores (HRV, RHR) are useful as long-trend personal baselines but unreliable as day-to-day fatigue measures — too many inputs shift them (sleep, cycle phase, daily routines, hydration, anything you ingested). Use the performance + symptom signals below as the primary read.

Green-light to add the planned wk 2/3 set:

  • Top-set RIR feels at-or-below the planned RIR (your subjective sense of "1 left" matches what you actually finished)
  • No new back signals — morning AM stiffness ≤ 5 min
  • Sleep weekly average ≥ 7.5 h
  • Sessions completing in planned time without rest-period inflation

Yellow — hold volume, don't add:

  • Same load feels 0.5–1 RPE harder for 2 consecutive sessions (Helms; Chiang 2025 on RPE drift as early fatigue signal)
  • Eccentric tempo cheating — you're rushing the 6-count on pull-up negatives without meaning to
  • Sleep weekly avg dipped below 7 h
  • Cycle irregularity in a non-cycle-monitored month → fuel signal (Mountjoy RED-S)

Red — pull the deload forward by a week:

  • Any new leg signal (per Signs to Check In About list in Framework)
  • Two consecutive sessions where you couldn't finish the planned top set at planned RIR
  • Morning back stiffness extending past 15 min for 3+ consecutive days
  • Hand-held hinge feels different — even subtly

🔀 Flare Week vs Scheduled Deload — Do They Double Up?

A flare-driven downshift counts as that mesocycle's deload. Resume the next block at MEV (wk 1 settings), not at wk 5 where you left off. Two consecutive downshifts in a 6-week window overcompensates and breaks tendon adaptation continuity.

Exception: if the flare-deload happens in wk 1 or wk 2, run a normal 4-week ramp from wk 3 and keep the scheduled wk-6 deload — the flare was too early in the block to count as adequate recovery.

Tracking: mark the calendar week the flare downshift occurred. If a scheduled deload would fall within ±1 week of a flare deload, skip the scheduled one. Daily Low-Load auto-de-escalates per Framework — the rule here is just to not double-deload accidentally.

  • Mesocycle 2 (wks 7–12): still 5 lb. Add density via 4 working sets, not load. Annular repair is biological (Adams & Roughley).
  • Mesocycle 3 (wks 13–18): 5 lb still — earliest goblet hinge trial is at PT visit during wk 12 deload. Goblet only on PT sign-off.

The contrast is the point. The BSS climbs in volume and reps inside the block. The hinge climbs in tempo and density only — the load number does not move for ~18 weeks, because the disc is the binding constraint and it doesn't care what week the calendar says.

// Non-Negotiable Rules

HYPERMOBILITY PROTOCOL

Joint hypermobility means the nervous system has learned to rely on passive structures — ligaments and joint capsules — instead of muscles. Every rule below actively retrains that pattern toward active stabilization.

Never Lock Out

Stop 10–15° before full extension — elbows, knees, hips. The click is capsular loading, not muscular control. That distinction matters for everything.

Warmup Every Session

Hypermobile joints need neural activation before load. This is where hypermobile athletes get hurt. No exceptions.

Proprioception Work

Eyes-closed balance + random perturbation retrain position sense. The nuance that matters for you: active position sense is good, passive end-range sense is the blind spot — stay actively engaged, never relax into end range.

Isometrics Are Medicine

Every hold in this program teaches muscles to fire without passive end-range joint reliance. The core retraining goal.

No Passive Stretching

No yin yoga. No passive splits. No hanging end-range. More passive range without control = more instability.

Tendons Take 8+ Weeks

Muscles adapt in 2–3 weeks. Tendons 8+. When something feels easy, wait 4 more weeks before adding load.

Closed-Chain Mid-Range

All hypertrophy progressions: closed-chain, mid-range, isometric → eccentric → concentric. Co-contraction cues, not feeling-the-stretch cues.

HRV in Trends Only

Use HRV / RHR as own-baseline trends, not absolute readiness scores. Many inputs shift baselines — sleep, cycle phase, hydration, anything pharmacologic you've added or changed. Day-to-day variance is noise; multi-week drift with no obvious cause is the signal worth acting on.

// Vegetarian Hypertrophy Fuel · Tailored Stack

NUTRITION

Protein density first, micronutrients second, low-sugar always. Per-meal protein dose 30g+ matters more than daily total — vegetarian sources have lower leucine, so absolute dose has to clear ~3g leucine to maximize muscle protein synthesis (Phillips & van Loon 2011, 2019).

110–120g
Protein Daily
≈2.2–2.5 g/kg — deliberately at the upper end, since under-eating (not over) is the risk for you (Phillips & van Loon 2019; ISSN 2017). The floor matters more than the ceiling: clear 100g every day before chasing 120. Per-meal dose by source: dairy/whey 25–30g (leucine-dense); tofu/lentil meals 35–40g to clear the ~2.5–3g leucine threshold (van Vliet & Burd). Dairy carries the leucine load — pair it with any tofu/lentil meal. 4 doses across the day.
1700–2100
Kcal Range
Floor 1700 on rest days; up to 2100 on lift + skill days. The risk for your body composition is under-eating, not over. Lean into adequacy — fat-dense foods (PB, avocado, walnuts, olive oil drizzle on savory) are the easy lever for a lighter athlete to hit the upper range without volume bloat.
5g
Creatine Daily
Vegetarians get an outsized response — baseline muscle creatine is lower without dietary meat (Dolan & Saunders 2019). Your product: Thorne Creatine, orange-pineapple. Shake into water or chocolate Fairlife. +16–24 oz water/day beyond normal — creatine pulls water into muscle, not optional. Optional 20g × 5–7d loading phase for faster saturation; 5g daily reaches saturation in 3–4 wks either way.
40g carbs
Pre-Workout
Real carbs ~60 min before training. Go-to: 2 rice cakes + 2 Tbsp nut butter, OR sourdough + sugar-free jam + chia. Not stevia-zone. LMNT or other electrolyte mix during the session — you sweat heavier than baseline and the sodium-loss compounds across a 5-day week. Don't train fasted.

32 mg
Iron Target (diet-first)

Pair non-heme iron with vitamin C at the same meal — bell pepper, lemon, kiwi, berries are all in your rotation. Skip coffee/tea within 1h. Supplement only if serum ferritin documented <30 ng/mL — get tested annually; routine supplementation without confirmed deficiency raises GI and oxidative-stress risk (Lonnerdal 2017).

Vitamin D3
Bone density + muscle function + active tissue recovery (Endocrine Society 2024 update). Indoor lifestyle and winter latitudes drop levels fast. Take with a fat-containing meal — your sourdough + PB breakfast is a natural pairing. Target serum 25(OH)D 40–60 ng/mL; check annually.
250+ mcg

Vegetarian-critical. Sublingual methylcobalamin — methyl form is the bioactive one and avoids the conversion step. Twice-weekly is more practical than daily and absorbs as well. Anchor it to the same mornings you do Daily Low-Load so you don't lose track.
200–400 mg
Magnesium (Bisglycinate)
Your product: Thorne Magnesium Bisglycinate. Bisglycinate form is better-absorbed and lower GI side effects than oxide/citrate. Supports muscle relaxation, sleep, and the cumulative-fatigue side of a 5-day week. Take with bedtime skyr — magnesium + casein evening snack stacks nicely for sleep onset. Loose stool = dose too high, drop by 100 mg.
1300–1500 mg
Calcium Daily
IOC consensus for female athletes is 1500 mg/d (Nieves; Gatorade SSI). Bumped from general 1000–1200 because: low body mass + vegetarian phytate/oxalate reduction + creatine training load on bone. Spread doses ≤500 mg/eating-occasion — above that, absorption % drops sharply (Heaney 1990). 3-way split: skyr AM (~300mg), calcium-set tofu OR fortified soy lunch (~250–350mg), Greek yogurt PM (~250mg) + FullWell. ≥2h apart from any iron supplement (Hallberg & Hulthén).
30–38g
Fiber Daily (split)
Target 30–38g total with ~10g soluble. Plant-heavy diet covers insoluble (kale, broccoli, sourdough, lentils). Soluble gap is the watch-item — chia (5g/Tbsp), oats, berries help. Consider 1 tsp psyllium husk in evening water 3–4×/wk — offsets daily-med slow-transit; 5–10g/day hits LDL-lowering threshold (Harvard Health; J Acad Nutr Diet 2017). Take with ≥12 oz water, never dry.
15–20g
Collagen + Vit C (pre-WO)
Pre-training, 30–60 min before. 15–20g hydrolyzed collagen or gelatin + 50 mg vitamin C doubled PINP (serum collagen-synthesis marker) in Shaw & Baar 2017; Aussieker 2023 suggests 30g may be superior to 15g. Disc + hypermobile + two-time annular-repair tissue = directly relevant for you. Mix into OJ OR pair with rice-cakes-and-PB + kiwi/berries for the C. Vital Proteins unflavored is widely used. Note: collagen is bovine/marine, not vegetarian — flag if that's a hard line.
500–1000 mg
Curcumin (bioavailable form)
Post-surgical chronic-inflammation maintenance. Form matters more than dose — plain turmeric powder is <1% bioavailable; use curcumin-with-piperine (Thorne Meriva-SF, NOW Curcumin + BioPerine) or liposomal/phytosomal. Take with fat-containing meal. Bonus: your South Indian curries (turmeric + black pepper + ghee/coconut oil bloomed in tadka) are the absorption-optimal combination dietarily — a daily dal with proper tadka adds meaningful effective dose. 4–8 weeks to evaluate (Daily et al. 2016 meta on chronic pain).
2–3g EPA+DHA
Omega-3 (vegetarian)
Vegetarian omega-3 conversion (ALA → EPA → DHA) is inefficient — supplement is non-optional for a 5-day training load. Pick: algal EPA+DHA (cleanest for vegetarian sourcing) OR Nordic Naturals ProOmega if you accept fish-oil. Food-based plant omega-3 sources you already eat — walnuts, chia, ground flax — help but don't replace the supplement. Take with a fat-containing meal.
200–400 mg

Mitochondrial function + tissue recovery. Take with lunch (fat-soluble). Form matters: ubiquinol > ubiquinone for absorption past age 30. Worth keeping continuously — no reason to cycle off.
Foundation

Covers methylated folate, methyl + adenosyl B12, 300 mg choline, 250 mcg iodine, 20 mg zinc, 200 mcg selenium, 4000 IU D3, K2 MK-7, 300 mg mag glycinate. Spinach, collards, bok choy, kale. Lightly cooked — raw oxalates bind calcium and reduce absorption. Splash of lemon while cooking = iron-vit-C pairing built in.

Hides, Richardson & Jull — Multifidus does not spontaneously recover after first-episode LBP; targeted retraining required. Why bird-dog goes daily, not weekly.
Stuart McGill — Spine-sparing principles, Big 3 (curl-up · side plank · bird-dog), endurance > strength for chronic spine. Anchors the Big 3 finisher and the no-loaded-flexion rule.
Adams & Dolan; Callaghan & McGill — Annular fiber failure under bending + compression at far lower loads than pure compression. Reason for permanent shelving of conventional deadlift and the SL-RDL deferral.
Schoenfeld et al. (2017, 2019) — Volume-hypertrophy curve; rep-range agnostic with proximity to failure. Justifies the volume cuts (lat raise 4×15→3×12, etc.) without strength penalty.
Petersen et al. (2011); van Dyk et al. (2019); Al Attar et al. — Petersen's RCT: ~60% reduction in new hamstring injuries and ~85% in recurrences with eccentric Nordic protocol. Meta-pooled effect across heterogeneous trials lands closer to ~51% (van Dyk, Al Attar). Why Nordic stays in the program despite its difficulty.
Reinold & Wilk (2007); Cools et al. (2014); Kibler & Sciascia — Lower trap MVIC peaks at prone Y, ~125° abduction, thumbs-up. Selection of the prone Y position over generic "Y-T-W" combos.
Distefano et al. (2009); Boren et al. JOSPT (2011); Selkowitz et al. (2013); Willcox & Burden JOSPT (2013) — Glute-med activation hierarchy: side-lying abduction with slight IR ~81% MVIC (highest); clamshell ~38% MVIC; the 30°-flexion clamshell finding (favorable glute-med-to-TFL ratio) traces specifically to Willcox & Burden. Why side-lying abduction is the hypertrophy driver and clamshell is the daily pattern groomer.
Adams & Roughley; Belavy et al. (2017, on running) — Disc tissue adapts to mechanical load; low-magnitude axial unloading and intermittent dynamic loading (walking, running) maintain hydration. Sustained sitting is the actual disc-degenerator. Anchors the daily decompression hang and walking emphasis over reverse hypers. Note: Belavy's specific paper is on running, not hanging — the hanging extension is consistent with the broader mechanism but is inference.
Russek (1999); Simmonds & Keer (2007); Engelbert; Scheper — Hypermobility-specific training: closed-chain, mid-range, isometric → eccentric → concentric, no end-range under load. Anchors all the depth caps and "never lock out" rules.
Snyder-Mackler; Buckthorpe et al.; Risberg et al. — Persistent quadriceps (especially VMO) deficit on the involved side post-ACL, often years out without targeted asymmetric work. Justifies the 25 left / 20 right banded TKE asymmetric warmup.
Lavagnino, Jenkyn (Spanish squat / decline-board work) — Vertical-shin patellofemoral-friendly quad loading reduces patellar tendon strain vs. forward-knee squat. Why Spanish squat is in the menu instead of more knees-forward variants.
Ekstrand; Bahr & Krosshaug; Mendiguchia — Sports injury epidemiology; eccentric overload for tendon resilience. Background for the slow-eccentric emphasis throughout.
Maeo et al. (2021) — Hamstring hypertrophy is greater with the hip flexed (lengthened) in the leg curl; gastrocnemius grows with the knee straight (standing > seated calf raise). Why the leg curl targets length and the calf raise is done standing.
Knee-extensor PFP systematic review (2025); Witvrouw OKC-vs-CKC 5-yr RCT — Leg extension is not contraindicated in patellofemoral pain; capping ROM to the 90→45° arc keeps load off the highest-stress range. Anchors the leg-extension reintroduction and the cap.
Rio et al. (2015) — Heavy isometric holds (5 × 45s) produce ~45 min of analgesia and cortical disinhibition. Why Spanish-squat isometrics lead the knee day.
Halabi et al. (2025) — Hip + knee strengthening beats knee-only for patellofemoral pain (large pooled effect). Why glute-med work is treated as knee medicine.
Contreras (2-1 method); Plotkin et al. (2023) — Extra unilateral volume on the weak side restores symmetry; hip thrust ≈ squat for glute-max hypertrophy. Anchors the right-glute 2-1 dosing and the hip-thrust selection.
Resistance-training dose-response & proximity-to-failure meta-analyses (2023–2024) — Volume drives hypertrophy with diminishing returns; failure isn't required (RIR 1–4); machines ≈ free weights for hypertrophy. Anchors the return-to-training volume ramp and the machine integration.
Wilke (1999) intradiscal pressure; diurnal disc-hydration MRI work — Seated hamstring contraction at 90° hip flexion drives posterior pelvic tilt / lumbar flexion; morning intradiscal pressure runs far higher. Why the leg curl is run prone, and why loaded flexion is kept out of the first hour awake.
